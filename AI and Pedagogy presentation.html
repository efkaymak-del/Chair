Level 1 · The Point

AI didn’t replace anything here.

It built infrastructure that would otherwise require a web developer and weeks of work. The instructor provides content expertise and learning design. The AI provides technical execution. The entire app runs client-side — no server, no database, no costs. Hosted free on GitHub Pages.

Level 2 · Skills Scaffolding

PSIR220 — Research Design

Students struggle with methodology. The gap between “I understand process tracing” and “I can write a process-tracing analysis” is enormous.

Solution: A paradigm-specific toolkit — every tool walks students through a thinking process, not content recall.

⚔️ Strategist

📖 Storyteller

🔥 Disruptor

Critical · Milliken · Corpus → discursive patterns → productivity & foreclosure → dominant account → positionality

Level 3 · The Key Move
“Your research goal is not ‘know everything about the country’ but ‘know enough to act like its delegation in this committee on this agenda.’” — PSIR172 MUN Preparation Workshop

The workshop teaches students the difference between a weak approach (capital city, population, flag) and a strong approach (role on the issue, pressures, allies, red lines, acceptable wording). AI built the scaffolding for an exercise that is fundamentally about doing, not knowing.

Level 4 · Philosophical Inquiry

PSIR308 — Contemporary Political Thought

Political theory is often taught as lecture → reading → essay. Students can define Rawls but can’t tell you what happens when Fanon interrogates him.

Solution: Scenario-based philosophical simulations where students apply theoretical lenses to dilemmas and discover their own commitments — then confront them in live debate.

Act 4 · Policy as Enabler

A Framework, Not a Ban

The goal is not to prohibit AI use but to make it transparent, intentional, and educationally purposeful. The Department Council (14 September, Karar 1) and FBE AI Policy both adopt this principle.

EU Trustworthy AI — 7 Principles

Human agency · Technical robustness · Privacy · Transparency · Diversity · Societal wellbeing · Accountability

Applied to Student Work

Every principle maps onto assessment: students must remain in control, be transparent about tool use, protect data, and take responsibility for their output.

Act 4 · Policy as Enabler

Three-Tier AI Use Taxonomy

Tier
What It Means
Disclosure Required
⚫ Green — Permitted
Grammar/spell-check, translation, search, accessibility tools
None
🟠 Amber — Permitted with Disclosure
Brainstorming, outlining, paraphrasing, data visualization, coding assistance
Full disclosure: tool, purpose, extent
🔴 Red — Not Permitted
Submitting AI-generated text as your own, using AI during closed-book exams, fabricating sources
Academic misconduct
Act 4 · Policy as Enabler

The Disclosure Statement

“In preparing this work I used [tool name] to [describe purpose — e.g., brainstorm outline, check grammar, generate data visualization]. After using this tool, I reviewed and edited the output and take full responsibility for the content of my submission.”

This language is drawn from the FBE AI Policy (v1.0) and will appear on all assignment submission pages.

Act 4 · Policy as Enabler

Provisional Syllabus Clause

AI Use Policy (Provisional): This course permits the use of AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini) for specified purposes, subject to the department’s three-tier classification. All Amber-tier uses must be disclosed using the standard disclosure statement. Red-tier uses constitute academic misconduct. This clause is provisional pending the Faculty Board’s final AI policy decision and will be updated accordingly.

Act 6 · Discussion

Over to You

Four prompts for open conversation. Take any one — or raise your own.

Discussion Prompt 1

The Assessment Question

If AI can produce a credible essay on any topic in our syllabi, what is a final exam actually measuring? Should we change what we assess, how we assess, or both?

Discussion Prompt 2

The Skills Question

Which skills become more important in an AI-enabled world? Which become less important? What does this mean for our program learning outcomes?

Discussion Prompt 3

The Equity Question

AI tools are not equally accessible. Some students have paid subscriptions, better devices, or more familiarity. How do we ensure our AI policy doesn’t deepen existing inequalities?

Discussion Prompt 4

The Identity Question

If an instructor can build 60 apps by talking to an AI, what does “expertise” mean in 2026? What does it mean to be a university lecturer when the lecture can be generated?
