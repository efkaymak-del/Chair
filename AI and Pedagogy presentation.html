Level 3 · The Key Move
"Your research goal is not 'know everything about the country' but 'know enough to act like its delegation in this committee on this agenda.'" — PSIR172 MUN Preparation Workshop

The workshop teaches students the difference between a weak approach (capital city, population, flag) and a strong approach (role on the issue, pressures, allies, red lines, acceptable wording). AI built the scaffolding for an exercise that is fundamentally about doing, not knowing.

Act 4 · Policy as Enabler

Faculty AI Policy — Version 1.0

Effective 2026–2027 · For Faculty Board review and adoption

Grounded in the EU Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI (High-Level Expert Group, 2019). Most university AI policies are ad hoc rules. Ours is anchored in an established, principled ethical framework — the same one the EU uses for AI regulation.

Four ethical imperatives: respect for human autonomy, prevention of harm, fairness, and explicability. From these, seven operational requirements.

Act 4 · The Seven Principles

EU Trustworthy AI → Student Academic Work

1. Human agency & oversight
You remain the author. AI may assist, but your judgment directs and checks every output.
2. Technical robustness
AI outputs can be wrong or fabricated. Verify facts, data, and citations before relying on them.
3. Privacy & data governance
Do not enter confidential or personal data into AI tools without authorization.
4. Transparency
Disclose when and how you used AI. Your work must be traceable and honestly attributed.
5. Diversity & fairness
Be alert to bias in AI outputs. Misuse for unfair advantage is prohibited.
6. Societal well-being
Use AI responsibly, mindful of its wider ethical, social, and resource costs.
7. Accountability
You are fully responsible for everything you submit, including any AI-assisted content.
Act 4 · The Three-Tier Taxonomy

Permitted · Authorized Only · Prohibited

Tier
Examples
Requirement
✓ Permitted

Act 4 · The Disclosure Requirement

The Model Statement

"In preparing this work I used [tool name] to [brief description]. I reviewed and verified all output, and I take full responsibility for the content of this submission."

Additive, not punitive. It normalizes transparency and gives instructors a foundation for conversation rather than accusation.

Act 4 · Syllabus Integration

The Provisional Clause

Per Department Council Karar 6 (14 September 2026):

Syllabi go to students the week of 22 September with three components finalized: learning outcomes in active verbs, assessment mapped to programme outcomes, rebalanced weights. The AI clause is marked provisional (geçici). Once the Faculty Board formalizes the policy, instructors update only that clause within one week.

Time without vacuum. Students know AI rules exist and are coming; instructors aren't forced to finalize before the institutional position is clear.

Act 4 · The Connection

Policy Enables Practice

Without a clear policy, colleagues are paralyzed — some ban AI entirely, others ignore it. A principled framework liberates everyone to experiment purposefully.

The same "human agency and oversight" principle that governs student use is what the instructor practiced in every app: AI builds, the instructor designs and decides.
